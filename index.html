QuickBooks AI Operations Assistant

An AI-assisted bookkeeping operations system for small bookkeeping firms. It reviews uncategorized expenses, catches exceptions, chases missing receipts and overdue invoices, and sends a weekly summary. A person stays in control of every decision that matters.

The business problem

Small bookkeeping firms lose hours every week on repetitive work that still needs professional judgment.

Uncategorized transactions

Expenses land in "Uncategorized Expense" and wait for someone to categorize them one by one.

Errors slip through

Duplicates, missing vendors and unusually large amounts are easy to miss in a long list.

Missing receipts

Expenses above the receipt threshold have no supporting document, and chasing clients is manual.

Overdue invoices

Customer follow-ups depend on someone remembering, so they're inconsistent.

No weekly view

The team lead has no single summary of what's waiting, what's overdue and what failed.

The constraint

Accounting judgment can't be handed to an AI. The system had to recommend, not decide.

How it works

One n8n workflow organised into lanes. Each lane runs on its own schedule and shares one configuration and one error-alert path.

LANE 1Weekdays 8:00 · 13:00

Transaction intake

Reads expenses waiting in QuickBooks' holding account and flags duplicates, missing vendor or description, large amounts and multi-line items.

LANE 1bWeekdays 8:15 · 13:15

Jev review & decision

Jev (the AI assistant) suggests a category from the real account list, with a confidence score and reasoning. Business rules decide what happens next.

LANE 2Every 2 hours

Approval sync

Sends approved categories to QuickBooks after re-checking the live expense. Only the category changes, and never twice.

LANE 3Weekdays 9:00 · 14:00

Receipts & requests

Matches $100+ expenses to receipts in Drive. Jev drafts requests for missing ones, and a person approves before anything is sent.

LANE 5Weekdays 9:30

Overdue invoices

Tracks past-due invoices, drafts polite reminders for approval, follows up after 7 days, escalates to a person, and detects payments.

LANE 6Mondays 7:45

Weekly report

Builds the week's figures from Airtable. Jev writes a short summary using only those numbers, and the team gets a "Needs a person" list.

Lane 4 is the crash alert: if a live run fails unexpectedly, the team gets an email with the execution ID, the failing step and the error.

The auto-approval gate

An expense skips human review only if all four conditions pass. Everything else goes to a person.

Built-in safeguards

  • Potential duplicates are flagged, never deleted or reversed
  • QuickBooks is re-read right before every update
  • Invalid reviewer input is refused, with a plain-language reason
  • Test mode sends every email to a test inbox

Full audit trail

  • Every run, AI review, QuickBooks update, email and error is logged
  • Failures are retried, then alert the team with cleaned messages (no tokens)
  • If the AI is unavailable, items go to a person

    Business impact: what is measured and what is not

    This is a portfolio build on fictional data, so I only claim what was measured.

    Measured in the sandbox

    • AI cost ≈ $0.001 per transaction reviewed
    • Weekly report ≈ 6 Airtable API calls, no QuickBooks calls
    • Scheduled runs stay within Airtable's free-plan API limit

    Not claimed, and how a firm would measure it

    • Minutes per expense, before vs. after
    • Share of AI suggestions approved vs. changed
    • Receipts collected per week; days-to-payment on reminded invoices

    All of these can be read from the system's Audit Log during a pilot.

    Known limitations

    • QuickBooks bank-feed "For Review" items aren't available through the API, so the system works on expenses posted to a holding account.
    • Multi-line expenses and credits are always left to a person.
    • The weekly AI summary can occasionally add an explanation that isn't in the data. The tables are the source of truth.
